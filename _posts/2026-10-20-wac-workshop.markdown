I will be doing a more thorough write-up of the paper once the proceedings are out, but the basic premise of the paper is this: Modern web corpora are massive, containing billions of documents and trillions of words. Yet, for any given research task, only a small fraction of the documents are relevant. This means that researchers often have to spend a lot of time filtering out irrelevant documents before they can start their actual research. In this paper, we propose a method for annotating web documents with task-agnostic descriptors generated by large language models (LLMs). These descriptors can then be used to filter the documents for any given research task, making it easier and faster for researchers to find the relevant documents.

While our method does not reach similar levels of accuracy as training task-specific classifiers, I do believe our method is of value for many smaller research projects, where annotating data and training custom classifiers is not feasible. The method is also useful for researchers who want to explore the data and get a sense of what it contains before committing to a specific research task.
